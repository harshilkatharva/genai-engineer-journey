Client Policy — Returns, Refunds & Exchanges
============================================
Synthetic returns policy with product-specific distinctions and timing traps.

CX-RET-01 — Standard return window
----------------------------------
The general consumer return request window is seven calendar days from recorded delivery, provided the product is eligible and the applicable product conditions are met. This is a request window, not an unconditional promise that every product can be returned.

CX-RET-02 — Unopened electronics
--------------------------------
Unopened, eligible electronics are generally easier to process because activation and usage state can be verified as unchanged. The exact product matrix still controls the final outcome.

CX-RET-03 — Activated phones
----------------------------
An activated phone is not automatically treated the same as an unopened phone. The support team checks product-specific activation conditions, account state, physical condition and the stated reason for return.

CX-RET-04 — Laptop inspection
-----------------------------
A laptop showing physical damage, liquid exposure or an altered internal component may require inspection before a return decision. Installed software alone does not automatically establish a return rejection, but the condition must be evaluated under the applicable process.

CX-RET-05 — Transit damage
--------------------------
Visible transit damage should be reported as soon as possible and preferably within 48 hours. Photographs of the packaging, shipping label and damaged area can help establish whether the case is transit-related.

CX-RET-06 — Missing accessory
-----------------------------
A return involving a missing adapter, remote, power cable or other included accessory may need a completeness check. The absence of an included accessory can affect the replacement or refund path.

CX-RET-07 — AC installation
---------------------------
Air-conditioner return eligibility can differ after installation because the product may have been altered by installation work and a site service may already have occurred. The customer should contact support before uninstalling or moving the unit.

CX-RET-08 — TV installation
---------------------------
A television that has been wall-mounted should not be treated as an unopened unit. The support team may need to coordinate de-installation and inspect condition before approving a return path.

CX-RET-09 — Exchange availability
---------------------------------
An exchange is subject to replacement inventory. Support should not promise the exact same model if inventory cannot be confirmed.

CX-RET-10 — Defective product
-----------------------------
A defective device may be routed through warranty or replacement handling rather than the normal change-of-mind return route. The correct route depends on the product, date, symptoms and applicable service coverage.

CX-RET-11 — Refund initiation
-----------------------------
Once the return is approved and required verification is complete, Virelia initiates the refund through the original approved payment route. Initiation is an internal processing event; the customer's bank-posting date can be later.

CX-RET-12 — Refund method
-------------------------
Refunds normally follow the payment route used for the original order. Exceptions require authorized handling and should be recorded in the case.

CX-RET-13 — Partial refund
--------------------------
A partial refund may be appropriate for an approved pricing adjustment or another case-specific resolution. It is not the standard outcome for a simple eligible full return.

CX-RET-14 — Return pickup
-------------------------
Pickup availability depends on geography, item size and carrier service. A return approval does not guarantee same-day pickup.

CX-RET-15 — Packaging
---------------------
Customers should retain original packaging until a return or damage case is resolved. Large appliances and televisions may require packaging or protective materials during transport.

CX-RET-16 — Wrong item
----------------------
If a customer receives a different product code from the one ordered, the case is treated as an order-fulfillment error. The support team should compare the order record, shipping label and physical product code.

CX-RET-17 — Serial mismatch
---------------------------
A serial number that does not match the dispatch record requires investigation. It should not be assumed to be a normal return because the physical unit cannot be reliably connected to the shipped item.

CX-RET-18 — Refund dispute
--------------------------
If the customer says the refund has not arrived, support should first establish whether Virelia initiated the refund and then provide the payment-reference information permitted by policy. The bank's posting time is outside Virelia's direct processing control.

CX-RET-19 — Return exclusion
----------------------------
Certain categories can carry product-specific exclusions due to activation, hygiene, custom configuration, installation or supplier terms. Exclusions should be stated from the current return matrix rather than guessed from category labels.

CX-RET-20 — Policy precedence
-----------------------------
A specific product return rule supersedes a generic statement when the specific rule clearly addresses the same product condition. A support answer that cites only the generic seven-day window may be incomplete.

CX-RET-21 — Example case
------------------------
A Samsung Vision QLED 65 delivered Tuesday is reported damaged Wednesday morning with photographs. The case should be routed as potential transit damage before a generic discretionary-return answer is given. The timing supports prompt reporting, but inspection still matters.

CX-RET-22 — Example case
------------------------
A customer activates the Pixel 10 Business phone, uses it for four days, and then requests a return because they changed their mind. The answer requires the phone-specific activated-device condition; the generic unopened-product logic cannot simply be copied.


CX-RET-23 — Example: accessory only
-----------------------------------
A customer wants to return VLT-PHA-USBC-65, an accessory purchased separately. The accessory return class is not automatically the same as a laptop return rule.


CX-RET-24 — Example: opened phone
---------------------------------
A customer opens the Samsung Galaxy A95 Business, signs in and uses it for two days, then asks for a change-of-mind return. The activated-device rule must be retrieved before answering.


CX-RET-25 — Example: wrong laptop code
--------------------------------------
An order contains VLT-LN-E14G6-16 but the customer receives VLT-DE-5450-16. This is a fulfillment mismatch, even if both devices have 16 GB RAM and 512 GB SSD.


CX-RET-26 — Example: installed AC
---------------------------------
A customer asks to return VLT-AC-BS-20EI after an installation crew has completed work. The case may require de-installation coordination and inspection rather than ordinary unopened-item handling.


CX-RET-27 — Example: damaged packaging only
-------------------------------------------
A carton is dented but the television itself appears undamaged. Support should record the evidence and assess whether the case is cosmetic packaging damage or a transit-damage claim requiring further inspection.


CX-RET-28 — Example: refund reference
-------------------------------------
A payment-reference number can show that a refund was initiated, but it should not be interpreted as proof of immediate bank settlement.


CX-RET-29 — Example: inventory unavailable for exchange
-------------------------------------------------------
A customer qualifies for an exchange but the exact replacement model is out of stock. The support team should not promise same-model replacement until inventory is confirmed.


CX-RET-30 — Example: serial mismatch
------------------------------------
A returned laptop serial differs from the serial recorded on the original order. The case should move to an identity and fulfillment investigation before final disposition.
