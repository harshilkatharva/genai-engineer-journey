HR — Conduct, Workplace & Employee Security
===========================================
Synthetic employee conduct policy with security boundaries.

HR-CON-01 — Professional behavior
---------------------------------
Employees must communicate respectfully with colleagues, customers, vendors and service providers. The policy applies during office work, remote work, travel, customer visits and company-sponsored events when the interaction is work-related.

HR-CON-02 — Conflict disclosure
-------------------------------
A conflict of interest exists when a personal relationship or financial interest could reasonably affect an employee's business judgment. Disclosure triggers review; it does not automatically establish misconduct.

HR-CON-03 — Gifts
-----------------
Supplier gifts must be handled under the gift-and-hospitality thresholds. Small promotional items may be permissible, while high-value gifts or benefits tied to a pending commercial decision require review and may need to be declined.

HR-CON-04 — Personal cloud storage
----------------------------------
Employees must not place confidential company files into personal cloud drives merely because the personal service is convenient. An approved transfer mechanism should be used when business data must move between systems.

HR-CON-05 — Generative AI use
-----------------------------
Public AI tools should not receive confidential company documents, customer credentials, private HR records, source code classified as restricted, or other protected material unless the company has approved that particular service and workflow. Convenience is not a data-classification exception.

HR-CON-06 — Device security
---------------------------
Company-managed devices must maintain required security tooling. Employees should contact IT when a security control blocks legitimate work instead of disabling the control themselves.

HR-CON-07 — Lost laptop
-----------------------
A lost company laptop should be reported through the security incident path as soon as possible. The incident may trigger remote-lock or account-protection actions. Waiting to see whether the device turns up can increase exposure.

HR-CON-08 — Shared accounts
---------------------------
Shared credentials reduce accountability and are prohibited for systems that support individual identity. Where a service truly requires a shared technical identity, the owner must use the approved secret-management mechanism.

HR-CON-09 — External USB devices
--------------------------------
Unknown USB media should not be connected to managed laptops. Security can provide a controlled process for examining external media where business need exists.

HR-CON-10 — Remote workspace
----------------------------
Remote workers should take reasonable measures to prevent unauthorized viewing of company information. Public spaces require particular caution when screens, printed material, or customer information are visible.

HR-CON-11 — Incident escalation
-------------------------------
A suspected security incident should be reported even when the employee is uncertain whether the event is malicious. Early reporting allows security to triage before evidence disappears.

HR-CON-12 — Customer information
--------------------------------
Customer addresses, contact details, order history and support notes are business information. Exporting a customer list to a personal spreadsheet for convenience is not permitted.

HR-CON-13 — Employee information
--------------------------------
Employee records contain personal information and should be accessed only for legitimate HR, management or operational purposes. Curiosity is not a business purpose.

HR-CON-14 — External communication
----------------------------------
Employees should not publicly disclose incident details, internal pricing structures, unpublished product information or confidential client information unless authorized by the relevant function.

HR-CON-15 — Password handling
-----------------------------
The company will not ask an employee to send a password or one-time code over email or chat. Security verification should use approved methods.

HR-CON-16 — Access termination
------------------------------
When an employee exits, system access is removed according to the separation workflow. Returning a laptop to HR does not itself prove that application access has been revoked.

HR-CON-17 — Personal devices
----------------------------
Bring-your-own-device use is allowed only for approved scenarios. A personal device does not automatically receive the same access rights as a company-managed endpoint.

HR-CON-18 — Records and investigations
--------------------------------------
Employees should preserve relevant evidence when a security or conduct review is underway. Deleting messages or files because they seem embarrassing can interfere with an investigation.

HR-CON-19 — Retaliation
-----------------------
Employees who raise concerns in good faith should not face retaliation for making the report. Investigation conclusions are separate from the protected act of reporting a concern.

HR-CON-20 — Policy hierarchy
----------------------------
Where a security control imposes a stricter requirement than a general convenience rule, the security control governs. For example, a manager's desire for immediate access does not override a required privileged-access approval.
