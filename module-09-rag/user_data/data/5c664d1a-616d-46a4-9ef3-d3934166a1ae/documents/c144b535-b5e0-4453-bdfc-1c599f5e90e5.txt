Security, Privacy & Data Retention Policy
=========================================
Synthetic information-security policy designed for retrieval, metadata filtering and policy-exception tests.

SEC-001 — Least privilege
-------------------------
Users receive the minimum access needed for their job. A senior title does not automatically justify privileged access. Access requests must identify the system, requested role and business reason.

SEC-002 — Manager approval
--------------------------
Most standard application access requests require manager approval. Privileged or production access can require additional Security or system-owner approval. The correct approval path depends on the requested privilege level.

SEC-003 — Emergency access
--------------------------
Emergency privileged access can be granted through the incident process when urgent response requires it. Emergency access must be time-bounded and reviewed afterward.

SEC-004 — Credentials
---------------------
Passwords, one-time codes, private keys and session tokens are sensitive. Employees must not paste them into support tickets, chat rooms or public AI interfaces.

SEC-005 — AI and confidential files
-----------------------------------
Employees may use approved AI workflows only with data categories permitted for that workflow. A public chatbot should not receive customer support exports, payroll files, unreleased contract information or internal credentials unless the approved system explicitly supports that data classification.

SEC-006 — Data minimization
---------------------------
Support and operational teams should collect only the information needed to solve the reported issue. More data is not automatically better for troubleshooting.

SEC-007 — Export controls
-------------------------
Large exports of customer or employee information may trigger additional approval and monitoring. A legitimate business purpose should be documented when the export is substantial.

SEC-008 — Device loss
---------------------
A lost managed device must be reported promptly so that Security can consider locking the device, revoking sessions and reviewing access. The employee does not need to wait until theft is confirmed.

SEC-009 — Malware suspicion
---------------------------
Unexpected browser redirects, disabled security tools, suspicious login prompts or unusual endpoint behavior can be reported as potential incidents. Users should not continue experimenting with untrusted software to determine whether it is malicious.

SEC-010 — Retention categories
------------------------------
HR records, payroll records, customer order records, support tickets, security logs and procurement files have different retention needs. A request to remove one record type does not automatically erase other records that have independent retention obligations.

SEC-011 — Legal hold
--------------------
When a legal hold or formal investigation applies, routine deletion may be suspended for relevant information. Employees should preserve the material identified by the hold notice.

SEC-012 — Access reviews
------------------------
Privileged access is periodically reviewed. A user who no longer needs a privileged role should be removed from that role even if the account remains active for ordinary work.

SEC-013 — Service accounts
--------------------------
Service accounts must have an owner, documented purpose and appropriate secret-management controls. An engineer's personal login should not be converted into a permanent service identity merely for convenience.

SEC-014 — Vendor access
-----------------------
Third-party technicians can receive temporary access only where the business need and security controls are satisfied. A supplier relationship alone does not justify unrestricted network access.

SEC-015 — Incident evidence
---------------------------
Security teams may need logs, timestamps, endpoint identifiers or message headers to reconstruct an event. Employees should not delete potentially relevant evidence once an incident is reported.

SEC-016 — Privacy requests
--------------------------
Data-subject requests are handled through a designated privacy process. Frontline support should avoid making legal conclusions about deletion or retention.

SEC-017 — Public disclosure
---------------------------
Internal incidents should not be described publicly without authorization. Public communication belongs to designated corporate functions.

SEC-018 — Remote access
-----------------------
Remote access should use approved authentication and company-managed controls. Unmanaged public computers are not an acceptable substitute for an approved endpoint when sensitive systems are involved.

SEC-019 — Data classification
-----------------------------
Public, internal, confidential and restricted data should be treated differently. Classification is attached to the information, not merely to the file extension or application used to open it.

SEC-020 — Security versus convenience
-------------------------------------
When a convenience request conflicts with a mandatory security control, security wins unless an approved exception path exists. An employee's urgency does not create an undocumented bypass.
