Client Policy — Purchase, Orders & Delivery
===========================================
Synthetic customer purchasing policy. Default terms can be overridden only by recorded customer-specific agreements.

CX-PUR-01 — Quote creation
--------------------------
A quotation records the requested products, quantities, commercial price, validity period, and any stated delivery or service assumptions. Creating a quotation does not automatically reserve inventory. If a customer needs a reservation, the quote must include or link to the reservation record.

CX-PUR-02 — Quote validity
--------------------------
The default quotation validity is seven calendar days. Some promotions use a shorter window. The quotation's own validity field controls the actual expiry date. Once expired, the sales team must confirm current price and availability rather than simply reusing the old amount.

CX-PUR-03 — Enterprise purchase orders
--------------------------------------
Enterprise purchase orders should contain the customer's legal entity, billing entity, shipping address, contact person, product code, quantity, commercial terms, tax information and purchase-order reference. If key fields are missing, the order team should request correction rather than infer sensitive commercial data.

CX-PUR-04 — Payment methods
---------------------------
Consumer orders can use approved digital payment methods. Enterprise customers with an approved credit arrangement can use purchase-order terms. An enterprise customer without approved credit must follow the available prepaid or otherwise authorized payment route.

CX-PUR-05 — Inventory reservation
---------------------------------
Inventory is reserved only when the relevant order process records a reservation. A salesperson writing 'hold these units' in a chat channel is not enough to establish a system reservation.

CX-PUR-06 — Backorders
----------------------
When a requested product is unavailable, the sales team may offer a backorder, an alternative model, or a later dispatch window. An alternative model is a new commercial choice and should not be substituted without customer agreement.

CX-PUR-07 — Delivery estimate
-----------------------------
A delivery estimate is a planning forecast. It becomes a commitment only when the order record explicitly identifies a committed date or service level. Weather, carrier disruption and regional constraints can affect forecast dates.

CX-PUR-08 — Installation appointment
------------------------------------
Installation for a TV or AC is scheduled separately from parcel handoff. A delivered product may therefore exist in the customer's possession while the installation appointment is still pending.

CX-PUR-09 — Address changes
---------------------------
A shipping-address change after dispatch may require carrier coordination and can create an additional charge. Customers should not assume that a support agent can edit the address after the carrier has accepted the shipment.

CX-PUR-10 — Partial fulfillment
-------------------------------
An order containing multiple product codes can ship in separate consignments when inventory differs. Partial delivery does not automatically cancel the undelivered items.

CX-PUR-11 — Commercial approval
-------------------------------
Discounts beyond the standard sales authority threshold require an approval record. The approval should identify the order or quote, discount amount, rationale and approver.

CX-PUR-12 — Tax information
---------------------------
Tax details must align with the customer's legal and billing information. If the purchase order contains inconsistent tax information, the order should be paused for clarification.

CX-PUR-13 — Customer acceptance
-------------------------------
Customer acceptance of a quote should be captured through the approved commercial channel. An unsigned conversation can be evidence of intent but is not automatically equivalent to the formal acceptance state.

CX-PUR-14 — Order amendment
---------------------------
Changing quantity, product code or destination after order creation may require a new approval or order amendment. A support ticket can document the request but does not itself alter the commercial order.

CX-PUR-15 — Cancellation before dispatch
----------------------------------------
A customer may request cancellation before dispatch, subject to order status and contract terms. Once shipment has entered a carrier process, the case can require a return-to-sender or return workflow rather than a simple cancellation.

CX-PUR-16 — Customer-specific terms
-----------------------------------
A negotiated enterprise agreement can contain different payment or delivery terms. Customer-specific terms should be recorded and linked to the account or order. They do not become a general rule for all customers.

CX-PUR-17 — Currency
--------------------
Standard catalog prices in this demo are expressed in Indian rupees. An enterprise agreement using another currency must identify the currency explicitly. The presence of a number without currency is insufficient evidence for a commercial answer.

CX-PUR-18 — Sales notes
-----------------------
Sales notes can explain context but should not be used to contradict a formal customer contract. When a discrepancy occurs, the account team should escalate for commercial review.

CX-PUR-19 — Delivery proof
--------------------------
Delivery confirmation is linked to the carrier or installation record as applicable. A courier scan proves a delivery event; it does not prove that every accessory was installed or that the customer has accepted every product condition.

CX-PUR-20 — Price mismatch
--------------------------
If a customer has a valid quotation and the order page shows a different amount, the sales team must identify whether the quote is still valid, whether a promotional condition changed, or whether the order contains a different configuration. The first visible price should not automatically be treated as the correct one.


CX-PUR-21 — Example: mixed-cart order
-------------------------------------
A customer orders a laptop, phone and installation service in one transaction. The product codes identify the hardware, while the service code identifies the installation. The order should not describe installation as a property of the laptop itself.


CX-PUR-22 — Example: expired promotion
--------------------------------------
A promotional quote expires after three days even though the default quotation rule is seven days. The quote's own validity field controls that promotion.


CX-PUR-23 — Example: customer asks for alternative model
--------------------------------------------------------
A customer asks for an alternative to an unavailable Dell laptop. Sales can propose the Lenovo or HP model, but the new product code and price must be shown before acceptance.


CX-PUR-24 — Example: partial dispatch
-------------------------------------
Two of five line items are available immediately. The customer accepts partial dispatch. The remaining three stay open unless the customer or contract specifies another treatment.


CX-PUR-25 — Example: wrong tax registration
-------------------------------------------
A purchase order lists a tax registration that conflicts with the customer's legal entity record. The order team should request clarification instead of silently choosing one value.


CX-PUR-26 — Example: delivery date promise
------------------------------------------
A salesperson says 'it will arrive Friday' during a call. Unless Friday is reflected as a committed date in the order, the statement should be treated as a planning communication rather than a formal guaranteed date.


CX-PUR-27 — Example: post-dispatch cancellation
-----------------------------------------------
A customer asks for cancellation after the package has entered carrier handling. The team may need to use the return-to-sender or return process rather than treating the order as never dispatched.


CX-PUR-28 — Example: contract term
----------------------------------
An enterprise contract provides 30-day payment terms while the public web storefront normally requires immediate payment. The account's recorded contract controls the enterprise transaction.
